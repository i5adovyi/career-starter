# Learning from actual career goals

Run `python career.py learn` after onboarding and a useful search. Treat its
output as a starting point for the assistant and candidate to review.

The local planner compares candidate-provided CV evidence and profile skills
with recognized requirements from review leads. Read `local/learning-plan.json`
for requirement excerpts, source jobs, candidate evidence and priority reasoning.
It supplies practical exercises and completion checks within the weekly budget.
An unknown skill starts with an assessment; absence from a CV is not a deficit.
The built-in vocabulary is finite, so inspect specialized or unrecognized
requirements yourself rather than treating the generated analysis as exhaustive.

Unmodified generated plans are refreshed when their inputs change. Existing
manual plans and progress notes remain intact; review a separately generated
`learning-plan.suggested*.md` shown in the portal before merging useful changes.
Do not copy skill assessments into the CV evidence ledger as verified ability.

Prioritize evidence in this order:

1. A specific interview or work-sample difficulty.
2. A repeated requirement in suitable current job descriptions.
3. An unknown skill that a brief conversation or practical task can assess.

Distinguish a skill gap from missing proof or unclear CV wording. Do not
prescribe a course solely because a keyword is absent from the CV. Keep market
sample size and uncertainty visible; one description does not establish a
general market requirement.

Use at most three active topics within the candidate's weekly time budget.
Each topic needs:

- A target-role requirement and the evidence for prioritizing it.
- The candidate's starting point: demonstrated, partial, unknown or absent.
- One practical task and a small first step.
- A concrete output and a check that shows whether the task was done well.
- A realistic time allocation and review point.

Choose outputs that suit the profession: a writing sample, spreadsheet,
design, customer scenario, language exercise, portfolio item, analysis,
presentation or technical project. Do not make a code repository the default
for every candidate.

Offer free or already accessible material first. Verify current sources when
recommending a particular course. Do not enroll, buy, or promise certification.
If details cannot be verified, provide a learning objective and practical task
instead of inventing a resource.

Plans, reading and attendance are not completion evidence. On completion,
record what the candidate did, the result, feedback and where the output lives
under `local/`. Label project/coursework experience accurately in any future
CV. Interview preparation can improve confidence without changing the
candidate's claimed qualifications.

Revisit the plan when target roles, available time, market evidence or interview
feedback change. A break or return to work does not justify assumptions about
ability, confidence, health or family responsibilities.
